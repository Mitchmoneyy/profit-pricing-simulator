What price elasticity means

Elasticity describes how strongly conversion reacts to price. At an elasticity of 1.0, a 10% price rise loses about 10% of conversions. Below 1, customers barely notice price changes, which is typical of niche tools, B2B software and trusted brands. Above 1.5, buyers compare prices closely, as with commodity products and impulse buys.

Conversion at new price = current conversion × (new price ÷ current price) ^ −elasticity
Monthly profit = new customers × contribution − ad spend − fixed costs

How to estimate your elasticity

The best estimate comes from your own history: compare conversion before and after a past price change, holding traffic quality steady. Without data, start at 1.0 for most consumer products and 0.6–0.8 for B2B or specialist products, then test a price change on new visitors and update the number.

Why the best price is often higher than you think

Every sale carries fixed costs: delivery, the $0.30 fee and the ad spend that brought the buyer in. A higher price spreads those over fewer, more profitable sales. Unless your market is very price-sensitive, the profit-maximising price usually sits above where founders start.

Frequently asked questions

Is the profit-maximising price the right price?

It is the price that maximises this month's profit under your elasticity estimate. You may choose a lower price to grow faster or win market share; the calculator shows what that choice costs.

Does raising prices increase refunds?

It can. If you expect higher refunds at a higher price, raise the refund rate when testing that price.

What if I sell a subscription?

The same logic applies to the monthly price. In the full simulator, switch to the subscription model to also see how churn changes lifetime value.

How accurate is this?

As accurate as the elasticity estimate. Treat the result as a direction and a size of opportunity, then confirm it with a real price test.
