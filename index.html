How it's calculated

Every number in the simulator comes from these formulas.

Fee per sale = price × fee% + $0.30
Net revenue = price × (1 − refund rate)
Contribution = net revenue − delivery cost − fee
New customers = visitors × conversion · CAC = ad spend ÷ new customers
Profit per sale = contribution − CAC
Monthly profit = new customers × contribution − ad spend − fixed costs
LTV (one-time) = contribution × orders per customer · LTV (subscription) = contribution × 1 ÷ churn, capped at 60 months
What is a good LTV:CAC ratio?

A common rule of thumb is 3× or better: each customer brings in three times what it cost to acquire them. Below 1× you lose money on every customer. Far above 5× often means you could spend more on growth.

How do I calculate my break-even price?

Divide your monthly ad spend and fixed costs by the customers you win each month, add delivery cost and the $0.30 fixed fee, then divide by (1 − refund rate − fee rate). The Break-evens card does this live.

What is contribution margin?

The share of each sale you keep after refunds, delivery or cost of goods, and payment fees, before ads and fixed costs. It shows how much each sale can put toward acquisition and overhead.

Why is my SaaS monthly profit so low?

Monthly profit counts only this month's new customers, so recurring revenue from existing subscribers is not included. The Cohort LTV section shows what each month's customers earn over two years.

Can I import numbers from Shopify, Stripe or Google Analytics?

Yes, as a screenshot or an export. Drop a dashboard screenshot, a CSV or JSON export, or paste report text. The importer looks for labels such as sessions, conversion rate, AOV, ad spend, refunds and churn, and asks you to approve each value.

Is my data private?

Yes. Calculations, screenshot reading and history all run in your browser. Snapshots and scenarios are stored on this device only; use Back up to keep a copy or move them to another device.

How does price elasticity work here?

Elasticity sets how conversion reacts to price. At 1.0, a 10% price rise loses about 10% of conversions. The price chart and the solver use it to find the price that maximises monthly profit.

How should I use this every month?

Update the inputs that changed, then save a snapshot. History draws profit, margin and CAC trends and warns you when margin drops 5+ points or CAC rises 20%+.
