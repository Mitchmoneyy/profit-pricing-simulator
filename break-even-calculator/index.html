How the break-even point is calculated

Each sale contributes what's left after refunds, delivery and payment fees. Break-even is the number of sales whose combined contribution covers your monthly ad spend and fixed costs.

Fee per sale = price × fee% + $0.30
Contribution per sale = price × (1 − refund rate) − delivery cost − fee
Break-even sales = (ad spend + fixed costs) ÷ contribution per sale
Break-even conversion = break-even sales ÷ monthly visitors

Worked example

An online store sells at $68 with $22 of product and shipping cost, a 2.9% + $0.30 card fee and 6% refunds. Each sale contributes $68 × 0.94 − $22 − $2.27 = $39.65. With $14,000 of ads and $9,000 of fixed costs, it needs $23,000 ÷ $39.65 = 581 sales a month to break even. At 42,000 visitors that's a 1.38% conversion rate, so at 2.1% the store clears break-even comfortably.

Three ways to lower your break-even point

Raise contribution per sale

A price rise, a cheaper supplier or fewer refunds all lower the number of sales you need. Contribution is the denominator, so small changes move break-even a lot when margins are thin.

Cut fixed costs

Software, salaries and rent are paid whether you sell or not. Every $1,000 of monthly fixed cost removed lowers break-even by $1,000 ÷ contribution sales.

Spend ad money more efficiently

Ad spend sits in the numerator. If the same customers can be won for less, break-even drops without touching price.

Frequently asked questions

What is a break-even point?

The level of sales at which revenue after variable costs exactly covers fixed costs and ad spend, so monthly profit is zero. Above it you make money; below it you lose money.

Should ad spend count as a fixed cost?

For a monthly break-even, yes: it is a budget you commit to each month. The full simulator also shows break-even per sale, where ad spend becomes customer acquisition cost.

Why is the $0.30 fee included?

Most card processors charge a percentage plus a fixed amount per transaction, commonly 2.9% + $0.30. On low prices the fixed part is a large share of the sale.

How is break-even price different from break-even sales?

Break-even sales holds your price fixed and asks how many customers you need. Break-even price holds your customer count fixed and asks what price makes the month profitable.
