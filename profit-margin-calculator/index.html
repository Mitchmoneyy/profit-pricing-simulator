The formula

Fee per sale = price × fee% + $0.30
Refund cost = price × refund rate
Contribution = price − refund cost − delivery cost − fee
Contribution margin = contribution ÷ price
Profit per sale = contribution − customer acquisition cost

The gap between the first and last line is why stores with healthy-looking gross margins can still lose money: refunds, fees and ads are paid on every order.

Typical contribution margins by business model

Rough ranges for an average business: e-commerce around 35%, agencies around 35%, creators and memberships around 65%, SaaS around 70%, online courses around 75%. Top performers run 15–20 points higher.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin for an online store?

It depends on the category, but a contribution margin of 40–55% before ads gives most stores enough room to pay for acquisition and still profit on the first order.

Do I include shipping in delivery cost?

Include whatever you pay to fulfil an order: product cost, packaging, shipping you pay for, and per-order platform or app fees.

Is profit per sale negative a problem?

Not always. If customers buy again or subscribe, lifetime value can cover the loss on the first order. Use the LTV:CAC calculator to check.

Where do I find my customer acquisition cost?

Divide last month's ad spend by the number of new customers it brought in. Many ad dashboards show this as cost per purchase or cost per acquisition.
